# Weather and Environment System

**Project:** Project Sovereign  
**Canonical Path:** `systems/world/weather-and-environment-system.md`  
**System:** World Simulation / Weather and Environment  
**Document Type:** Canonical Behavioural Specification  
**Document ID:** ENV-001  
**Status:** Canonical initial specification; climate/weather datasets, numerical models, schemas and calibration remain open  
**Starting World Date:** 1 January 1955  
**Technology Horizon:** At least 2026, extensible beyond

---

## 1. Purpose

The Weather and Environment System governs time-dependent atmospheric, climatic and environmental conditions that influence activity in the simulated world. It provides spatially and temporally scoped environmental state to Movement and Transportation, Sensors and Detection, Equipment, Combat, Logistics, Population and other systems.

The system must support long-term climate patterns, seasonal variation, local weather, severe events and changing environmental conditions at the appropriate level of simulation detail. It must distinguish broad climate from current weather, environmental observation from actual environmental state, and environmental conditions from persistent terrain-object state.

**Core principle: Environment supplies conditions; consuming systems calculate their own consequences.** Weather may affect movement speed, sensor performance, aircraft operations, sea state, equipment reliability, human exposure or supply handling, but Environment does not directly calculate every domain effect or apply a universal penalty. It supplies the relevant conditions and transitions; each domain applies its own validated rules.

The system must support the historical starting date of 1 January 1955 and later technological eras without assuming modern forecasting, observation or mitigation capabilities exist from the start.

## 2. Design Principles

1. **One environmental truth:** the system owns authoritative environmental state at its declared spatial and temporal resolution.
2. **Climate and weather are distinct:** climate describes long-term distributions and seasonal patterns; weather describes conditions over a particular time and place.
3. **Conditions are not consequences:** Movement, Sensors, Equipment, Combat and other domains apply their own effects.
4. **Terrain state remains distinct:** weather may cause or contribute to muddy, icy, flooded or other terrain transitions, but Terrain Objects owns the persistent terrain state and its transition history.
5. **Spatial and temporal scope are explicit:** every environmental value must specify its area/cell/region, effective time or interval, unit and validity.
6. **Uncertainty is represented:** forecasts and observations are not identical to actual environmental state.
7. **No universal weather modifier:** effects depend on the specific condition, domain, equipment, activity and operating threshold.
8. **Historical capability matters:** forecasts, sensors, communications and environmental mitigation depend on technology, institutions and infrastructure.
9. **Causal transitions:** environmental changes have a source or process, effective time and resulting state; terrain damage or repair is not silently overwritten by weather.
10. **Player and AI parity:** both use the same environmental truth and the same information/forecast rules.
11. **Deterministic persistence:** weather evolution and state transitions must be reproducible under save/load and event replay.
12. **Scalable fidelity:** the simulation may use regional or grid-based approximations, but resolution and limitations must be explicit.

## 3. Scope and Ownership

### 3.1 Weather and Environment Owns

- Actual atmospheric/weather conditions at the configured simulation resolution.
- Climate baselines, seasonal patterns and configured long-term environmental distributions.
- Weather evolution and environmental state transitions within the implemented model.
- Spatial and temporal interpolation or aggregation of environmental conditions.
- Environmental forecast products generated by the simulated forecasting capability, including uncertainty.
- Environmental observation products where produced by this system, while specialised sensors may own their measured observations.
- Environmental event records for storms, precipitation, extreme temperature, fog, icing, drought, flooding drivers, sea state and other supported phenomena.
- Shared environmental condition definitions, units, bounds, persistence and validity.
- Interfaces by which domain systems query actual conditions and actor-available forecasts.

### 3.2 Owned Elsewhere

- Geographic geometry, coordinates, elevation data and spatial indexing: World Map.
- Terrain-object identity, geometry, persistent terrain state and terrain-state history: Terrain Objects.
- Roads, bridges, railways, ports, airfields and other infrastructure identity/condition/capacity: Infrastructure and Facilities and applicable domain systems.
- Actual movement rates, route feasibility and traversal: Movement and Transportation.
- Detection probability, sensor performance, contact tracks and classification: Sensors and Detection.
- Equipment reliability, serviceability, damage and operating constraints: Equipment and Materiel.
- Weapon engagement, ballistics and combat damage: Combat and Engagement.
- Human health, injury, exposure, nutrition and mortality: Character/Medical/Population systems as defined by their specifications.
- Crop yields, industrial processes, power generation and economic losses: Economy, Production, Population and other domain systems.
- Environmental policy, emissions and long-term human-driven changes where modelled: Government/Policy and relevant environmental simulation rules.
- Actor knowledge, communications and intelligence assessment: Communications, Sensors and Intelligence.
- Environmental mitigation actions such as road clearing, drainage, de-icing, shelter, repair or protective equipment: the relevant Engineering, Infrastructure, Equipment, Military or civilian systems.

## 4. Core Concepts

### 4.1 Climate

Climate is a long-term statistical description of environmental conditions for a region or spatial unit. It may include seasonal distributions of temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity, cloudiness, sea state or other supported variables.

Climate is not the current weather. It informs weather generation and long-term expectations but does not itself establish the actual condition at a specific time.

### 4.2 Weather State

Weather State is the actual environmental condition at a defined location or region and simulation time. Each variable must have a canonical identity, unit, range, effective time, spatial resolution and any relevant quality/confidence metadata.

The initial model may support a subset of:
- Air temperature.
- Precipitation type and intensity.
- Wind speed and direction.
- Humidity or moisture.
- Cloud cover and ceiling.
- Visibility, fog and haze.
- Atmospheric pressure where used by a domain.
- Snow cover, snowfall and blizzard conditions.
- Ice, freezing precipitation and icing conditions.
- Thunderstorms, lightning and severe wind.
- Sea state, waves, currents, water temperature and related maritime conditions where modelled.
- Soil moisture, surface wetness, temperature and freeze/thaw drivers.
- Dust, sandstorms, smoke or other airborne obscurants where supported.

This list is an extensible candidate set, not a claim that every variable is implemented. Each variable must be included only when its definition, units, generation and consumers are specified.

### 4.3 Environmental Event

An Environmental Event is a time-bounded or state-changing phenomenon such as a storm, heatwave, blizzard, flood-producing rainfall, drought period, icing event or other configured condition.

An event may affect multiple spatial regions and variables. It must have a stable identity, time interval, spatial scope, intensity or relevant state, source/process and resolution status. An event does not automatically damage infrastructure or change terrain state; those consequences are resolved by the owning systems.

### 4.4 Environmental Forecast

A Forecast is an estimate of future environmental conditions available to a specified actor at a specified issue time. It must distinguish forecast issue time, target time/interval, spatial scope, expected values, uncertainty and forecast capability.

A forecast is not the actual future weather and must not overwrite actual Weather State. Forecast accuracy depends on available technology, observation coverage, processing, time horizon and configured model quality.

### 4.5 Environmental Observation

An Environmental Observation is a measurement or report from a weather station, ship, aircraft, sensor, human observer, satellite or other valid source. It may be incomplete, delayed or uncertain.

Where a specialised sensor system owns the measurement, Environment consumes the observation only through its interface. Actual environmental state and observed/known state remain distinct.

### 4.6 Terrain State Transition

Weather may trigger or contribute to a persistent Terrain State transition, such as Normal to Muddy, Icy, Snow-Covered, Flooded or another supported state. Terrain Objects owns the persistent terrain state and records the transition.

Environment supplies the relevant driver and timing. Terrain/Infrastructure or another responsible system evaluates whether the condition crosses the required threshold and commits the state transition. When weather clears, terrain does not necessarily revert instantly; drying, thawing, drainage, repair or other recovery may take time.

### 4.7 Environmental Exposure

Exposure is the condition experienced by a person, unit, platform, facility or cargo over a period. Environment supplies the conditions and exposure intervals. Character/Medical, Equipment, Logistics, Population and other systems calculate consequences for their own entities.

A single environmental value must not directly overwrite health, fatigue, equipment serviceability or cargo condition without a domain-owned transition.

## 5. Spatial and Temporal Representation

### 5.1 Spatial Resolution

Environmental conditions may be represented by a grid, region, altitude layer, maritime area or another configured spatial structure. The World Map provides the authoritative geometry and spatial query foundation.

The environment schema must specify the spatial resolution, coordinate/region reference and interpolation rules for each variable. A coarse regional condition may be used for strategic simulation, while tactical movement, aviation, sensor and combat systems may require finer detail.

The simulation must not imply precision beyond the configured resolution. If a variable is unknown at a requested location, the query returns an explicit unknown/estimated result or a documented interpolation result.

### 5.2 Temporal Resolution

Weather changes over simulation time. The system may update different variables at different intervals according to their dynamics and required fidelity. Resolution cadence must be explicit and consistent with the shared simulation clock.

A severe storm can change during an operation. A forecast created earlier remains an earlier forecast even after the actual weather changes.

### 5.3 Altitude and Vertical Structure

Where aviation, radar propagation, atmospheric effects or other systems require it, the model may represent conditions at different altitudes or vertical layers. Surface weather must not automatically be assumed to describe conditions at all altitudes.

The initial implementation may defer detailed vertical weather, but the schema must leave room for it without redefining the meaning of existing surface variables.

### 5.4 Interpolation and Boundaries

When a unit or platform moves between environmental cells or regions, the system must use a defined interpolation or transition rule. Region boundaries must not create arbitrary discontinuities unless the underlying model intentionally represents a front or other physical boundary.

A domain may query environmental conditions at a position, route segment, facility or time interval. The response must include units, effective time, resolution and uncertainty where relevant.

## 6. Weather Evolution and Environmental Events

### 6.1 Evolution Model

The weather model may use historical/climatological data, deterministic rules, stochastic generation or a more detailed physical model. The chosen approach must be explicit, reproducible and appropriate to the simulation's fidelity.

A simplified model is acceptable if it preserves spatial/temporal coherence, plausible persistence, seasonal patterns and meaningful environmental effects. It must not generate unrelated random weather independently for every unit or cell unless that is an explicitly validated abstraction.

### 6.2 Seasonal and Long-Term Variation

Climate and seasonal patterns inform expected conditions over time. Long-term environmental change, where enabled, must have an explicit owner, causal inputs and temporal model. It must not be silently introduced as an arbitrary drift in every environmental variable.

### 6.3 Severe Events

Severe events may have start, growth, peak, movement, decay and end phases where relevant. Their spatial footprints and effects evolve over time. A single event may affect movement, sensors, equipment, facilities, population and logistics differently according to each domain's rules.

The environmental event itself does not decide whether a bridge collapses, aircraft is lost, ship is damaged, person becomes ill or road becomes impassable. It supplies the conditions; domain systems evaluate thresholds and resolve consequences.

### 6.4 Determinism and Randomness

If weather evolution uses stochastic processes, random streams or event seeds must be deterministic and persisted according to shared simulation rules. Save/load must not regenerate a different storm solely because a calculation is repeated.

Randomness may be used to represent unresolved physical uncertainty, but it must not bypass causal inputs or create hidden advantages for AI.

## 7. Forecasting, Observation and Information Boundaries

### 7.1 Actual State Versus Forecast

Actual Weather State is the simulation's world truth. A Forecast is a dated estimate of future state. An Observation is evidence about actual or past conditions. These records must not be conflated.

Forecast updates create new versions or forecasts. They do not rewrite prior forecasts or make actors retrospectively aware of information they did not have.

### 7.2 Forecast Capability

Forecast capability may depend on weather stations, ships, aircraft, satellites, communication infrastructure, research, computational capacity and institutional procedures. The available capability is historically dependent.

A nation in 1955 must not automatically possess modern global satellite observation or digital forecasting. Forecast detail, horizon, accuracy and dissemination must be constrained by actual configured capability.

### 7.3 Actor Knowledge

The simulation may store actual weather state centrally, but each actor's usable forecast or observation must be delivered through permitted systems. Communications governs delivery; Intelligence or the relevant information system governs assessment and actor-specific knowledge where required.

An AI must not query perfect future weather merely because the Environment system can generate it. AI planning uses the same forecast and observation limits as the Player.

### 7.4 Uncertainty

Forecasts and observations may include uncertainty, confidence, range or qualitative quality. The model must define their meaning. Uncertainty must not be reduced to an arbitrary modifier that is applied identically across all domains.

## 8. Domain Interfaces and Environmental Effects

### 8.1 Movement and Transportation

Movement queries conditions relevant to route feasibility and actual travel, such as precipitation, surface wetness, ice, snow, wind, visibility, sea state, currents and other mode-specific variables.

Movement determines the resulting movement rate, route feasibility, delay, interruption or operating restriction. Environment must not directly move units or set their speed.

Weather may contribute to persistent terrain changes. Terrain Objects commits the terrain-state transition, and Movement evaluates the current state.

### 8.2 Sensors and Detection

Sensors may consume visibility, cloud cover, precipitation, fog, atmospheric conditions, sea state or other variables relevant to the specific sensor modality and geometry. Sensors owns detection probability, measurement, track quality and uncertainty.

Environment must not directly create or destroy contacts or set detection outcomes.

### 8.3 Equipment and Materiel

Equipment may consume temperature, moisture, dust, icing, sea state, corrosion conditions or other variables relevant to reliability, performance, damage and serviceability. Equipment owns the resulting equipment state and effects.

Environmental exposure may contribute to wear or failure through explicit equipment rules. Environment does not independently set equipment serviceability.

### 8.4 Combat and Engagement

Combat may consume environmental variables affecting visibility, sensor performance, weapon employment, ballistics, flight, sea operations, mobility, blast or other effects where supported by the relevant combat model.

Combat owns engagement eligibility, weapon/target interaction, damage and combat outcomes. Environment supplies the conditions and does not apply a universal combat penalty.

### 8.5 Logistics and Sustainment

Logistics may consume environmental conditions for route risk, transport feasibility, cargo handling, storage requirements, spoilage, fuel demand or expected delivery time. Movement owns actual movement execution. Inventory owns stock condition and quantity transitions. Logistics must not directly destroy or spoil cargo merely because a weather event occurred; a defined storage, exposure or transport rule must resolve the effect through the authoritative owner.

### 8.6 Character, Medical and Population

Relevant environmental exposure may influence health, fatigue, hydration, injury risk, work capacity, crop yields, population wellbeing or displacement where supported. Character/Medical/Population and other domain systems own those outcomes.

Environment provides the exposure conditions and duration. It must not duplicate person-level health or population state.

### 8.7 Infrastructure and Facilities

Weather may contribute to flooding, erosion, ice, wind damage, water levels, road/rail degradation, runway closure or other facility/network effects. Infrastructure owns the facility/network condition and any resulting damage, closure or repair state. Weather does not silently restore or destroy infrastructure.

### 8.8 Economy, Production and Industry

Environmental conditions may affect agricultural yields, resource extraction, industrial operations, energy generation, transport services, labour availability or costs where those mechanisms are defined. Production and Economy calculate their own output, capacity and financial consequences. Environment supplies conditions, not company revenue or economic state.

## 10. Time, Persistence and Determinism

### 10.1 Shared Simulation Clock

Weather state, events, forecasts and observations use the shared simulation calendar. The system must preserve effective time, issue time and forecast target time as separate fields where relevant.

### 10.2 Event Ordering

When environmental changes and domain actions occur in the same simulation interval, event ordering must be explicit. For example, weather deterioration, a route traversal step and a bridge closure must not produce contradictory movement outcomes.

Terrain or infrastructure state changes triggered by weather must be committed by the owning systems and then consumed by subsequent domain resolution according to deterministic event ordering.

### 10.3 Save/Load

Persist the current weather state, active environmental events, relevant climate/model configuration, forecast versions and unresolved transitions needed to resume the simulation. On load, do not regenerate already committed events or apply the same terrain/infrastructure driver twice.

### 10.4 Versioning

Climate data, weather variables, forecast models, generation rules and environmental event definitions must be versioned. Changes to units or semantics must not silently reinterpret saved world state.

## 11. Failure Modes and Edge Cases

The system must handle at least:

- Environmental value unavailable at a requested location/time.
- Forecast issued after the decision time or already stale.
- Actual weather diverges from a prior forecast.
- Weather observation is delayed, incomplete or erroneous.
- Region/cell boundary interpolation produces conflicting values.
- Severe weather crosses multiple regions and affects different modes differently.
- Weather changes while a unit or carrier is moving.
- Terrain becomes muddy/icy/flooded but must persist until a valid recovery transition.
- Infrastructure damage or closure is caused by weather and must be committed by its owner.
- A domain requests a variable that is unsupported in the current fidelity/configuration.
- A historical weather observation/forecast technology is unavailable at the simulation date.
- AI attempts to use actual future weather instead of available forecast information.
- Environmental effects are accidentally applied twice by both Environment and a consuming system.
- Save/load during an active storm or state transition.
- Stochastic weather evolution changes after event replay.
- A weather event is removed or revised by data updates without preserving history.
- Environmental conditions affect cargo only through a defined Inventory/Logistics rule.
- A weather-driven terrain transition conflicts with a repair or engineering action.
- A UI presents forecast data as actual state.
- A missing environmental value is silently interpreted as favourable conditions.

Each case must resolve through an explicit value, uncertainty, rejection, state transition or unsupported result. Missing environmental data must not default to perfect visibility, calm seas, dry roads or unlimited operating conditions.

## 12. Minimum Acceptance Tests

1. Climate baseline and actual weather state are stored as distinct concepts.
2. A forecast records issue time and target time and does not overwrite actual future weather.
3. A later weather change does not rewrite what an actor's earlier forecast contained.
4. A unit's movement outcome changes only through Movement rules consuming the relevant environmental conditions.
5. Weather can contribute to a terrain-state transition, but Terrain Objects commits and records the persistent state.
6. Clearing precipitation does not automatically clear muddy or flooded terrain without the configured recovery rule.
7. Sensors uses relevant environmental conditions to resolve detection; Environment does not directly create contacts.
8. Equipment consumes environmental exposure through its own reliability/serviceability rules.
9. Combat resolves environmental effects through its own engagement model without a universal hidden penalty.
10. Logistics cannot silently spoil or destroy cargo without an authorised Inventory/Logistics transition.
11. Infrastructure condition/closure changes are committed by Infrastructure or its designated owner.
12. A regionally scoped storm evolves coherently across adjacent cells/areas under the chosen model.
13. A weather query with unsupported resolution returns explicit unknown/estimated state rather than favourable defaults.
14. AI cannot access actual future weather unless the scenario explicitly grants it.
15. Historical forecast capability is constrained by available technology, sensors, communications and institutions.
16. Save/load during an environmental event preserves current state and does not duplicate terrain or infrastructure transitions.
17. Stochastic weather generation is reproducible under the configured simulation seed/event model.
18. Player and AI use the same actual weather state and actor-available forecast rules.
19. Environmental state is queried using the shared World Map spatial reference and correct units.
20. A consuming domain cannot apply the same environmental effect twice because both it and Environment attempt to own the consequence.

## 13. Open Decisions

- Initial environmental variable catalogue and canonical units.
- Spatial resolution and interpolation for strategic, operational and tactical use.
- Weather generation model, historical climate inputs and stochastic calibration.
- Forecast horizon, accuracy, uncertainty and historical availability by country/date.
- Altitude-layer representation for aviation and sensor propagation.
- Maritime currents, sea state, tides, water levels and their spatial/temporal models.
- Soil moisture, freeze/thaw, snow cover, flood and terrain-transition thresholds.
- How weather drivers trigger persistent Terrain State changes and how recovery is calculated.
- Environmental exposure contracts for Character/Medical, Equipment, Logistics and Population.
- Long-term climate change and human-driven environmental modification, if enabled.
- Event ordering with movement, combat, infrastructure damage and repair.
- Save/load representation and model/data versioning.
- Performance limits for high-resolution weather in large worlds.
- Integration with forecast observation sources, Communications and Intelligence.

Until these are resolved, the implementation must expose only supported variables and return explicit unknown/unsupported results rather than inventing precision or favourable conditions.

## 15. Completion Criteria

Weather and Environment is ready for implementation when:

1. The initial variable catalogue, units, spatial/temporal resolution and uncertainty semantics are stable.
2. World Map, Terrain, Movement, Sensors, Equipment, Combat, Logistics and Infrastructure interfaces agree on ownership.
3. Actual weather, observations and forecasts are distinct and historically bounded.
4. Weather-driven terrain/infrastructure changes are committed by the correct owners and persist/recover through explicit transitions.
5. Environmental effects are calculated by consuming domains, not by a universal Environment penalty.
6. Save/load and deterministic event replay preserve active weather and downstream state transitions.
7. At least one land-movement, sensor-detection and logistics scenario passes under changing weather.
8. Player and AI use the same environmental truth and forecast-access rules.

The logical environmental state, variable, forecast, observation and event contracts are defined in SCHEMA-WORLD-CMD-001. The initial variable catalogue, units, spatial/temporal resolution, climate/weather model, calibration and executable downstream-effect tests remain open. The existence of this specification does not prove that weather or environmental simulation has been implemented.

## Command Ops 2 Integration Contract (2026-10-27)

Environment informs planning through actor-accessible forecasts and observations while owning actual environmental state. Actual state, observation and forecast remain distinct products with provenance and effective times.

- Command planning may use only forecasts available to that actor at decision time, preserving issue time, horizon and uncertainty where represented. Never substitute future actual weather for a forecast.
- Movement, equipment, sensors, communications, logistics and combat calculate their own domain-specific effects; no universal weather penalty replaces these mechanisms.
- Environment may identify a driver or request a terrain transition, but the authoritative Terrain/Infrastructure domain commits persistent physical state.
- Material changes should be observable by affected systems and may trigger reassessment. Environment does not issue/cancel orders. Use thresholds and deterministic rules to prevent route/replan thrashing.
- Unsupported variables or resolution return unknown/unsupported rather than fabricated precision.

Required tests: forecast differs from actual conditions while the decision retains its original information basis; severe weather invalidates a route without editing the order; environment-driven state transitions survive replay without duplicate application.
